# The Never-Ending Journey of Water

The water you drink today may once have been part of a cloud, a glacier, or even the ocean. Earth's water is always on the move, switching between liquid, ice, and invisible vapor. The sun keeps it all going.

Here's how the trip works. First, the sun warms oceans, lakes, and rivers, and some of the water floats up into the air as vapor. Plants add more by breathing out water through their leaves. Higher up, the air is cooler, so the vapor turns into tiny droplets that bunch together into clouds. When the droplets get heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. That water then runs over the land into streams and lakes, or soaks into the ground. Finally, it collects in oceans, lakes, ice, and underground, ready to start the trip again.

Nearly 97 percent of Earth's water sits in the salty oceans.
