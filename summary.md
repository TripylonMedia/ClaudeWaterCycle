# The Never-Ending Journey of Water

The water in your glass may once have floated in a cloud or sat in an ocean. Earth's water is always on the move, and the sun keeps it going.

First, the sun warms oceans, lakes, and rivers, and some water rises into the air as invisible vapor. This is evaporation. Next, plants add more by letting water out through their leaves. High in the sky, the air is cooler, so the vapor turns into tiny droplets that gather into clouds. When the droplets get heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. That water then flows over the land into streams and lakes, or soaks into the ground. Finally, it collects in oceans, lakes, ice, and underground, ready to start the trip again.

Here's a fun fact: water vapor usually stays in the sky for only about 8 to 10 days before falling back down!
